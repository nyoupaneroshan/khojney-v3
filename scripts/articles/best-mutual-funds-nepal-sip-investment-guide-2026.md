# Best Mutual Funds in Nepal 2026: SIP Investment, Returns & How to Invest

Mutual funds are professionally managed collective investment schemes that pool capital from thousands of retail and institutional investors to build diversified portfolios of equity shares, government treasury bills, corporate bonds, and fixed deposits. For individuals looking to benefit from Nepal's economic growth without having to analyze balance sheets or time daily stock fluctuations on the [NEPSE stock market](/nepse), **mutual funds in Nepal** and **Systematic Investment Plans (SIP)** provide an optimal, low-risk entry vehicle.

In this guide, we break down how mutual funds operate in Nepal, compare open-ended versus close-ended schemes, explain how to establish automated monthly SIPs, and examine key metrics to evaluate top fund managers.

---

## How Mutual Funds Work in Nepal

In Nepal, mutual funds are structured under the **Mutual Fund Regulation 2067**, enacted by the Securities Board of Nepal (SEBON). Each mutual fund structure involves three main entities:

1. **Sponsor**: A reputed commercial bank (e.g., Nabil Bank, NIC Asia Bank, Global IME Bank, Sanima Bank).
2. **Fund Manager**: A specialized merchant banking subsidiary (e.g., Nabil Investment Banking, NIC Asia Capital).
3. **Fund Supervisor**: Independent senior professionals who ensure that the fund adheres strictly to investment mandates and regulatory bounds.

## Open-Ended vs Close-Ended Mutual Funds in Nepal

Understanding the differences between fund formats is crucial when planning your investment horizon:

| Feature | Open-Ended Mutual Funds | Close-Ended Mutual Funds |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maturity Period** | Perpetual (No maturity date) | Fixed lifespan (typically 5, 7, or 10 years) |
| **Unit Trading** | Bought/sold directly via Fund Manager at current NAV | Traded on the NEPSE secondary market like ordinary stocks |
| **Fund Size** | Dynamic (Grows or shrinks based on subscriptions) | Fixed total units established at IPO |
| **SIP Availability** | Yes, automated monthly/quarterly SIPs supported | No SIP; bought in lump sums via secondary market |
| **Redemption** | Instant redemption with fund manager | Liquidated at maturity or sold to another buyer on NEPSE |
| **Popular Schemes** | Nabil Balanced Fund, NIC Asia Dynamic Debt, Siddhartha Systematic | NIBL Samriddhi Fund, Global IME Balanced Fund |

## The Power of SIP (Systematic Investment Plan) in Nepal

A **Systematic Investment Plan (SIP)** allows you to invest a fixed sum of money (e.g., NPR 1,000, NPR 2,500, or NPR 5,000) into an open-ended mutual fund scheme at regular monthly or quarterly intervals.

### Why SIP is Ideal for Nepali Salaried Professionals:
1. **Rupee Cost Averaging**: When the NEPSE index drops, your fixed installment buys more units at a lower Net Asset Value (NAV). When the market rallies, your existing units appreciate. This eliminates the stress of timing market peaks and troughs.
2. **Compounding Returns**: Reinvesting dividends through Dividend Re-Investment Plans (DREP) enables exponential compound growth over 10 to 20 year horizons.
3. **Disciplined Financial Habit**: Automatic standing instructions linked with your [commercial bank account](/banks) ensure consistent monthly savings before discretionary spending.

## How to Start a Monthly SIP in Nepal Online (Step-by-Step)

Starting an open-ended SIP takes less than 10 minutes:

### Walkthrough:
1. **Choose an Open-Ended Scheme**: Popular open-ended schemes include *Nabil Balanced Scheme*, *NIC Asia Dynamic Debt Fund*, *Siddhartha Systematic Investment Scheme*, and *Kumari Sunaulo Yojana*.
2. **Visit the Fund Manager's Digital Portal**: For instance, navigate to the SIP portal of Nabil Investment Banking or NIC Asia Capital.
3. **Complete Online KYC**: Enter your Demat account BOID (16 digits), mobile number, email, and upload a copy of your citizenship certificate.
4. **Configure SIP Details**:
   - Monthly investment amount (minimum typically NPR 1,000).
   - Tenure (e.g., 5 years, 10 years, or unlimited).
   - Opt into the **Dividend Re-Investment Plan (DREP)** for automated compound growth.
5. **Set Up Payment**: Pay your first installment using ConnectIPS, eSewa, or Khalti. You can also configure standing automatic debit instructions through your bank.

## Key Metrics to Analyze Before Choosing a Mutual Fund

Before allocating capital, evaluate these quantitative indicators:

### 1. Net Asset Value (NAV)
The NAV represents the per-unit market value of all underlying assets owned by the fund minus liabilities. While newly launched funds start at a base NAV of NPR 10.00, high-performing funds trade at higher NAVs reflecting accrued capital gains and dividend reserves.

### 2. Expense Ratio & Management Fees
SEBON caps management and supervisory fees. Lower expense ratios mean a greater proportion of net earnings are retained and distributed to unit holders.

### 3. Asset Allocation Mix
Inspect the portfolio fact sheets published monthly by the fund manager. Look for a healthy mix across high-dividend commercial banks, resilient hydropower producers, fixed deposits, and cash equivalents.

## Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

### What is the minimum amount required to start a SIP in Nepal?
Most open-ended mutual fund schemes allow you to start with as little as **NPR 1,000 per month**.

### Are mutual fund dividends taxed in Nepal?
Yes. Under current tax laws, cash dividends distributed by mutual funds are subject to a final withholding tax of 5% for individual investors.

### Can I withdraw or cancel my SIP anytime?
Yes. Open-ended mutual funds provide full liquidity. You can pause your monthly SIP, alter installment amounts, or redeem your accumulated units at the prevailing weekly NAV without penalty fees once the initial lock-in period (if any) passes.
