# Brand Guidelines & Voice Development Reference

A comprehensive guide for defining, developing, and auditing brand voice, personality archetypes, tone spectrums, and linguistic standards.

---

## 2. The 4-Dimension Tone Spectrum

Define where your brand sits on each of the 4 primary communication spectrums:

### A. Formality
- **Formal (100-75)**: Academic, precise, third-person, strict syntax.
- **Professional & Confident (74-50)**: Polished, business-ready, clean, direct (ideal for B2B SaaS).
- **Conversational (49-30)**: Warm, contractions allowed, second-person "you", approachable.
- **Casual (29-0)**: Slang, emojis, colloquialisms, punchy fragments.

### B. Humor
- **Dry / Serious**: Straight to the point, data-focused, no jokes.
- **Light / Subtle**: Occasional witty analogies, gentle cleverness, never at the expense of clarity.
- **Playful / Irreverent**: Memes, self-deprecating banter, high personality.

### C. Cadence & Rhythm
- **Brisk & Punchy**: Short sentences (8-14 words). Bullet points. Scannable.
- **Narrative & Fluid**: Rhythmic balance of short and long clauses (14-20 words). Storytelling.
- **In-Depth & Analytical**: Deep contextual explanations with comprehensive clauses.

### D. Perspective
- **1st Person Singular ("I")**: Personal founder brands, solopreneurs, individual thought leadership.
- **1st Person Plural ("We / Our")**: Team-driven, collaborative, company voice.
- **2nd Person Direct ("You / Your")**: User-centric, coaching, addressing the customer's problem.
- **3rd Person ("The Platform / Users")**: Formal documentation, whitepapers, objective case studies.

## 3. Brand Voice Matrix Documentation Template

When setting up a brand voice, fill out this matrix:

```markdown
### Voice Attribute: [e.g. Authoritative yet Approachable]
- **What it means**: We share deep industry insights with data, but explain them in plain English.
- **Always do**: Back claims with evidence; explain acronyms on first use; use "you" and "we".
- **Never do**: Use pretentious jargon, condescend to beginners, or sound robotic.

### Voice Attribute: [e.g. Action-Oriented]
- **What it means**: Every piece of content should leave the reader with a clear next step.
- **Always do**: Start sections with strong action verbs (Build, Optimize, Deploy).
- **Never do**: Leave conclusions open-ended without a definitive takeaway or CTA.
```

## 4. Vocabulary & Style Rules

### Words to Embrace
- Clear, energetic verbs: *streamline, accelerate, master, uncover, build, evaluate, transform*
- Actionable adjectives: *practical, reliable, proven, measurable, concrete*
- Customer-centric nouns: *solution, workflow, outcome, growth, impact*

### Words to Avoid (Banned / Buzzwords)
- Hollow buzzwords: *synergy, revolutionary, best-of-breed, paradigm shift, ninja, guru*
- Overused fillers: *basically, literally, honestly, frankly, simply*
- Passive apologies: *we hope you like, maybe you can try, sort of, kind of*

---

## 5. Brand Voice Audit Checklist

Before releasing content, verify:
- [ ] Primary archetype clearly recognizable in the tone
- [ ] Formality matches audience expectations
- [ ] No banned buzzwords or empty corporate jargon
- [ ] Active voice used in at least 80% of sentences
- [ ] Flesch Reading Ease score fits target demographic (60-70 for general B2B/B2C, 50-60 for technical)
- [ ] Clear call to action (CTA) aligned with brand mission
